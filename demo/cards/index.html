Lantern

Hold the bonus.
Prove the price.

A liquidation pays a bonus computed from whatever price the feed published. Lantern holds that bonus back, so anyone can prove the price was false before the money is gone.

The problem

A feed's price decides what a loan is worth.

the feed prints a price
the market liquidates on it
the bonus is paid on that price

What the contract checks

Two sources have to agree within five per cent.

20% — as far as one report is allowed to move
5% — as far apart two sources may disagree

A print that disagrees with the second source beyond the tolerance is the evidence itself. Neither source has to be trusted, because the disagreement is measured, not judged.

The hold

The bonus is held for five minutes. The debt is not.

Repayment and the closing of the position still happen at the moment of liquidation, exactly as they did before. What becomes provisional is the profit.

When nobody contests the window, the liquidator is paid in full.

The proof

Anyone can challenge, and the contract decides.

A challenger supplies the rule and the evidence. The verdict is recomputed in the contract at adjudication, so neither side has to be believed.

Upheld: the borrower gets the seized collateral back, the liquidator keeps their principal and loses the profit, the prover is paid out of the feed's bond, and the feed must carry twenty per cent more collateral before it prices anything again.
